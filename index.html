Research

From methods to materials

I work on the theory and simulation of functional materials at the nanoscale. I develop quantum-mechanical methods, mainly based on density functional theory, write them into open-source codes, and use them to answer the questions that experiments and devices pose: why a material magnetises along one axis and not another, how two magnetic layers couple through a spacer, which crystal a molecule will form. The aim is to turn that understanding into design rules for more efficient and sustainable materials.

2005 – 2024

Past work

During my PhD at ICMM I implemented a fully relativistic pseudopotential formalism in SIESTA, bringing spin-orbit coupling to large-scale calculations with atomic orbitals.

At the University of York, in projects funded by Seagate, Samsung and Toyota, I applied it to the magnetic anisotropy of L10 FePt alloys, nanoparticles and Fe/MgO interfaces, the materials behind high-density recording and MRAM, and to permanent magnets. I linked these calculations to atomistic spin models to reach device sizes and temperatures.

At ICN2 in Barcelona I added constrained non-collinear magnetism to SIESTA and explained the antiferromagnetic coupling mediated by graphene in Fe/graphene/Co, a route to synthetic antiferromagnets with a spacer one atom thick. At Southampton I led three work packages of the ERC Synergy project ADAM on crystal structure prediction, where I built the treatment of molecular flexibility and validated it in the seventh international blind test.

2024 – today

Current work

At ICMM-CSIC I lead the development of computational methods along four lines:

  • Molecular flexibility in crystal structure prediction, for molecules that change shape as they pack.
  • Magnetic metal-organic frameworks, porous crystals in which magnetism, structure and chemistry can be designed together.
  • Geometry of quantum state space, and how it shapes the response of a material.
  • Automated, reproducible calculations with AWESOME, which sets up and documents every SIESTA calculation.

Alongside, I study Heusler alloys for spintronics, high-entropy alloys for permanent magnets, and the optoelectronic properties of MOFs and covalent organic frameworks with experimental groups at the institute.

Next

Future work

The aim is to turn these methods into design rules. For framework materials, that means predicting flexible and magnetic MOFs that respond to a stimulus before they are synthesised, so that actuation and sensing can be engineered from the structure up.

For spin-orbit materials, it means using quantum-geometric quantities from relativistic first-principles calculations as descriptors for spintronic and low-power devices.

Both rely on large, automated sets of calculations analysed with data-driven tools, and on keeping the methods open and reproducible so that non-specialists can use them with confidence.

AWESOME

In development · paper in preparation

Automated Workflow for Electronic Structure calculations Of MatErials

Calculations with numerical atomic orbitals reach large systems at modest cost, but their reliability rests on a chain of coupled choices: pseudopotentials, basis orbitals, integration mesh, Brillouin-zone sampling and, for magnetic and correlated materials, Hubbard parameters and magnetisation directions. These choices are usually made by hand, rarely tested for the system at hand and seldom recorded.

AWESOME is a Python package that makes them automatically for each system and records every decision.

  • A setup tailored to the system. Pseudopotentials for all species are optimised together, as soft as possible while transferable in the actual chemical environment; mesh and k-points are converged and the basis is built for those pseudopotentials.
  • One setup, used throughout. Relaxation with bond and powder X-ray diffraction analysis, linear-response Hubbard U, magnetic anisotropy along symmetry-adapted directions, constrained magnetic states, and orbital- and spin-resolved bands and densities of states.
  • Validated and traceable. A validation mode compares the setup with established pseudopotential tables, and every SIESTA run is stored with its exact inputs in a provenance database, so any result can be traced and reproduced.

The goal is to make reliable localised-basis calculations accessible to non-specialists and reproducible by others. Test cases include FePt, NiO and bcc Fe.
