Research

My work sits between method development and materials design. I write the theory into open-source electronic-structure codes, then use it to answer questions that experiments and devices pose: why a material is magnetic along one axis and not another, how two magnetic layers talk to each other through a spacer, which crystal a molecule will form.

2005 – 2024

Past work

During my PhD at ICMM I derived and implemented a fully relativistic pseudopotential formalism in the SIESTA code, bringing spin-orbit coupling to large-scale density-functional calculations with atomic orbitals.

At the University of York I applied it to the magnetic anisotropy of L10 FePt alloys, nanoparticles and Fe/MgO interfaces, the materials behind high-density magnetic recording and MRAM, in projects funded by Seagate, Samsung and Toyota. I linked DFT to atomistic spin models to reach device-relevant sizes and temperatures.

At ICN2 in Barcelona I added constrained non-collinear magnetism to SIESTA and explained the antiferromagnetic coupling mediated by graphene in Fe/graphene/Co, which opened a route to synthetic antiferromagnets with a spacer one atom thick. At the University of Southampton I led three work packages of the ERC Synergy project ADAM on crystal structure prediction, where I built a method that treats molecular flexibility explicitly and validated it in the seventh international blind test.

2024 – today

Current work

At ICMM-CSIC I lead the development of computational methods in materials science along three lines.

  • Molecular flexibility. Crystal structure prediction for molecules that change shape as they pack, one of the open problems of the field.
  • Magnetic metal-organic frameworks. Porous, tunable crystals in which magnetism, structure and chemistry can be designed together.
  • Geometry of quantum state space. How the geometric properties of electronic states shape the response of a material.

Alongside, I study Heusler alloys for spintronics, high-entropy alloys for permanent magnets, and the optoelectronic properties of MOFs and covalent organic frameworks with experimental groups at the institute.

Next

Future work

The aim is to turn these methods into design rules. For framework materials, that means predicting flexible and magnetic MOFs that respond to a stimulus before they are synthesised, so that actuation and sensing can be engineered from the structure up.

For spin-orbit materials, it means using quantum-geometric quantities computed from relativistic first-principles calculations as descriptors for spintronic and low-power devices.

Both directions rely on large sets of calculations analysed with data-driven tools, and on keeping the methods open and reproducible for the community.

Methods and software

I am an official developer of two open-source codes, and most of the methods I write end up in one of them.

SIESTA

Density-functional code for efficient electronic-structure calculations and molecular dynamics with numerical atomic orbitals. I develop its relativistic and magnetic functionality and teach in its schools.

Website GitLab Documentation

mol-CSPy

Crystal structure prediction for molecular crystals, developed in the group of Prof. Graeme Day at the University of Southampton. I develop its treatment of molecular flexibility.

What I have built

Spin-orbit coupling

Fully relativistic pseudopotential formalism for atomic-orbital basis sets, giving spin-orbit splittings and magnetic anisotropies in systems of hundreds of atoms.

J. Phys.: Condens. Matter 24, 086005 (2012) · Phys. Rev. B 104, 195104 (2021)

Constrained non-collinear magnetism

Constraint of the direction of atomic magnetic moments under a fully relativistic Hamiltonian, to map out magnetic energy landscapes.

J. Phys. Mater. 1, 015010 (2018)

DFT+U with spin-orbit coupling

Hubbard correction compatible with non-collinear magnetism and spin-orbit coupling, for strongly correlated magnetic materials.

Comput. Phys. Commun. 286, 108684 (2023)

Flexible crystal structure prediction

First-principles mapping of thousands of molecular conformations to select those most likely to crystallise, before generating crystal structures. Validated in the seventh CCDC blind test.

Acta Cryst. B 80, 517 (2024)
